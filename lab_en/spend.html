Does a country's rank by the index match its rank by military spending? Here are the two orders compared for every country on the site: where the index puts a country noticeably lower or higher than its budget does.

Gap = MPI rank − spending rank. +9: the index puts the country 9 places lower than its spending; −15: 15 places higher. This compares two orders; it is not an assessment of an army's effectiveness: the index measures resources and readiness, not battle results.

What cannot be concluded

  • The link is partly circular: military spending (E01, E02) is inside the index through the ECO pillar. The “Combat” column computes the gap on the combat pillars only (GRD, AIR, NAV, C4I, PER), without the economy.
  • The index measures resources, not results. “Result above spend” does not mean the army is more effective in battle; “spend above result” does not mean the money was spent badly.
  • Spending is in current dollars (SIPRI, E01). Where prices and wages are low (for example Egypt, Iran) a budget buys more; the “PPP” column recomputes the gap with spending at purchasing power parity (E02).
  • Nuclear forces are in spending but the nuclear axis is not in the conventional index: for nuclear states (NW badge) the gap leans towards “spend above result”.
  • Wartime spending (Ukraine) is not comparable with peacetime budgets.
  • For countries with small budgets the spending ranks are tightly packed, so the gap is noisy; such deviations are marked “fragile” when the index interval or the combat variant does not confirm them.
  • The order does not depend on κ (the conversion of the index into conventional power units): κ is not determined by the data.

How it is computed

The definitions were fixed before the calculation and not changed afterwards. The comparison is made inside each set (rank 1 is the largest value; ties take the average rank). A notable gap is 15% of the set or more. “Holds” means the deviation has the same sign at both ends of the index interval (p5 and p95, others unchanged) and in the combat variant. Spending is the 2024–2025 value; older values stand in the 2025 slice as the last available one (DPRK — 2018, Afghanistan — 2021) and are not compared. This is a descriptive tool, not a hypothesis test: there is no verdict on whether a country spends “rightly or wrongly”.
